### Three simple ways to use this bundle

**1. Print and color together.**
Sit beside your loved one — not across from them. Coloring shoulder-to-shoulder feels like company. Sitting across feels like being tested.

Use the Activity Tracker on Page 6 to mark which days you colored together. Over weeks, you'll see patterns — which subjects make them light up, which days they engage most.

# PAGE 4 — 5 TIPS FOR CAREGIVERS

<br>

# 5 Tips That Make Coloring Easier

<br>

[ INSERT IMAGE 4: Five light bulbs in an arc, largest with a heart inside. Center on page. Resize to ~5 inches wide. ]

<br>

### 1. Use thick markers or crayons — not thin pencils
Larger tools are easier to grip for arthritic hands or weakened fingers. Crayola Twistables, Mr. Sketch markers, or Faber-Castell jumbo pencils all work beautifully.

### 2. Sit beside them, not across from them
Side-by-side feels like company. Across-the-table can feel like being watched or evaluated. This small change makes a huge emotional difference.

### 3. Never correct
Going outside the lines is part of the joy. Choosing "wrong" colors is creative freedom. Praise every choice. Smile at every page. There are no mistakes here.

### 4. Keep sessions short
10–20 minutes is often perfect. Stop *before* they get tired or frustrated, not after. Ending on a high note makes them want to do it again tomorrow.

### 5. Play soft familiar music in the background
Music from their younger years (40s, 50s, 60s) often opens doors that words cannot. Frank Sinatra, Patsy Cline, Nat King Cole, Elvis — pick what fits their era. Soft volume only.

<br>

> Bonus tip: Take a photo of one finished page each week. You're building a memory book, one page at a time.

# PAGE 5 — MEMORY CONVERSATION PROMPTS

<br>

# 10 Memory Conversation Prompts

<br>

[ INSERT IMAGE 5: Two speech bubbles connected by dotted line, with camera and smile inside. Center on page. Resize to ~5 inches wide. ]

<br>

Use these one at a time as you color together. There's no rush. Don't push. Just ask gently, then listen. The pages give your hands something to do while their heart finds its words.

<br>

**1.** What was your favorite flower in the garden growing up?

**2.** Tell me about your first car. What did it look like?

**3.** What did your kitchen smell like on Sunday mornings?

**4.** Did you have a pet as a child? What was its name?

**5.** What was your favorite song to dance to?

**6.** What's a meal you remember your mother making?

**7.** Where did you go on your honeymoon?

**8.** What was the best birthday you ever had?

**9.** What's a place that always made you feel happy?

**10.** If you could go back to one summer day, which one would it be?

<br>

> Remember: Sometimes they'll talk for an hour. Sometimes they'll just smile. Both are perfect.
